# Solitaire vs Mahjong Solitaire: Which One Should You Play?

Klondike Solitaire and Mahjong Solitaire share a name and a reputation: calm, single-player, no timer, perfect for a quiet session. But put them side by side and they are almost different genres. Klondike is a card game about hidden information and careful exposure. Mahjong Solitaire is a tile game about visual scanning and match order. Both are excellent, both are free in your browser on SnackArcade, and both deserve a place in your rotation — but the one you should open depends on your mood. This guide compares them honestly so you can choose.

## The Rules: Cards vs Tiles

Klondike Solitaire uses one deck of cards. You build the seven tableau columns downward in alternating colours, move Aces to the foundations, and build each foundation up by suit from Ace to King. The challenge is that most of the tableau is hidden face down, so a big part of the game is deciding which hidden cards to expose and when. [Play Klondike Solitaire](/games/klondike-solitaire/) to see how that feels.

Mahjong Solitaire uses a set of decorated tiles arranged in a layout. You remove identical pairs, but only when both tiles are free — nothing on top, at least one open side. Nothing is hidden from you; every tile is visible from the start. The challenge is entirely about order: which pair to match first so that the board keeps unlocking instead of locking itself. [Play Mahjong Solitaire](/games/mahjong-solitaire/) and you will feel the difference immediately.

## The Core Skill: Planning vs Scanning

The two games exercise different mental muscles. Klondike is a planning game under hidden information. You cannot see the face-down cards, so you are reasoning about probabilities and deciding which exposure is worth the risk. It rewards patience and a feel for how the stock behaves. Mahjong Solitaire is a visual scanning game with perfect information. Everything is visible, so the skill is seeing the free pairs, recognising the trapped ones, and choosing the order that unlocks the most. It rewards observation and forward planning about the layout itself.

## Difficulty and Luck

Klondike Solitaire has a real luck element: not every deal is winnable, and the draw-three mode hides two cards between every available one. Statistically, only a fraction of deals can be won with best play, which is part of the game's charm — and part of its frustration. Mahjong Solitaire is more forgiving in some ways: with a shuffle available, almost every layout can be finished, and there is no hidden information to fight. But the board can still trap you if you match the wrong pairs early, so the difficulty is real even though it is fairer.

## Session Length and Pace

Klondike games are medium-length: most deals take five to fifteen minutes, and a bad deal can be abandoned in seconds. Mahjong Solitaire games are longer by nature — clearing a large layout takes a good while — but each individual match is quick, so you can dip in and out without losing the thread. In practice, Klondike feels like a focused puzzle you complete, while Mahjong feels like a slow, meditative project you return to. If you have ten minutes, Klondike fits better. If you have an evening, Mahjong rewards the longer session.

## Which One Should You Play?

Choose Klondike Solitaire when you want a real puzzle with hidden information, a clear goal, and the satisfaction of a well-played deal — especially if you enjoy the rhythm of cards and the challenge of the draw-three mode. Choose Mahjong Solitaire when you want something calmer, more visual, and more forgiving, where nothing is hidden and the pleasure is in the matching itself. And if you are still deciding, the honest answer is to play both: they are different enough that they do not compete for the same session, and they are both free.
