# Five Letters Guide: How to Win the Word Puzzle

Five Letters gives you six attempts to guess a hidden five-letter word. You type a real word, press Enter, and every letter turns green, yellow or grey. Green means the letter is correct and in the right position. Yellow means it is in the word but in the wrong place. Grey means it is not in the word at all. That is the entire rule set, and it is also a trap: the rules are so simple that players think the game is about vocabulary. It is not. It is about information. The players who win consistently are the ones who use each guess to eliminate possibilities, not the ones who know more words. Open [Play Five Letters](/games/five-letters/) and try this approach on your next game.

## Spend Your First Guess on Five Different Letters

Your opening guess has one job: return as much information as possible. A word with a repeated letter wastes a slot, because a second copy of the same letter tells you nothing new. Openers such as CRANE, SLATE or AUDIO test five separate letters at once and will almost always return at least one colour. Do not worry about guessing the answer on turn one. The opening is not there to win — it is there to narrow the field from thousands of words to a few dozen.

## Cover Vowels Early, But Not All of Them

English words are built around vowels, so your first guess should include at least two of them. Three or more vowels crowds out the consonants, and consonants are what actually separate candidate words. Consider the difference between guessing AUDIO and guessing QUIET. Both cover vowels, but AUDIO also rules in or out five different consonant positions. The sweet spot is two vowels plus three high-frequency consonants such as S, T, R, N or L. That combination covers the most ground in a single attempt.

## Yellow Means Move It, Not Remove It

The most common mistake in Five Letters is putting a yellow letter back in the same position out of habit. A yellow tile has already told you that position is wrong, so re-testing it throws away one fifth of your next guess for zero new information. When you get a yellow letter, your next word must place it somewhere else. Keep a mental list of where each yellow letter has been ruled out, and make sure your next guess obeys every constraint on the board — green letters stay where they are, yellow letters move, grey letters disappear.

## When Stuck, Guess a Word You Know Is Wrong

If you have four green letters and five possible endings, do not guess the endings one at a time — that costs up to five attempts. Instead, play a word that tests several candidate letters simultaneously, even though it cannot be the answer. For example, if the pattern is CR_NE and your candidates end in A, E, I, O or U, guess a word that uses several of those letters at once. One sacrificed guess beats three failed ones, and it turns a desperate gamble into a controlled elimination.

## Watch for Double Letters Late

Many players assume the answer contains five different letters. That assumption is wrong often enough to cost games. Words such as ABBEY, SPOON and TRUSS have a repeated letter, and the game does not mark the second copy any differently. If you have eliminated most of the alphabet and nothing fits, a repeated letter is very often the reason your candidate list looks empty. Keep a few double-letter words in mind for the late game, and be ready to test for them.

## Read the Keyboard, Not Just the Grid

The on-screen keyboard greys out every letter you have already ruled out. That sounds trivial, but it prevents the single most frustrating way to lose a game: submitting a word that contains a letter you personally eliminated two turns ago. Before you press Enter, glance at the keyboard. If any letter in your guess is grey there, change the word. The grid tells you about past guesses; the keyboard tells you about the present.

## Play It with a Strategy

The game rewards patience far more than speed. There is no clock, so the only pressure is the pressure you create by rushing. On your next round, open [Play Five Letters](/games/five-letters/), use a five-different-letter opener, and before every guess check your word against every colour already on the board.
