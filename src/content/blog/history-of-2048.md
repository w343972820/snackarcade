# The History of 2048 and Why It Went Viral

Every so often a browser game appears that seems to be everywhere at once. 2048 is one of those games. It was created in a single weekend by one person, released as open source, and within its first week it had drawn roughly four million visitors. A decade later it is still one of the most played puzzle games on the internet, and it spawned an entire genre of merge puzzles. This post tells the story of how that happened, and why the same open approach means you can still [play 2048 online](/games/2048/) today with no download and no sign-up.

## A Weekend Hobby Project

2048 was created by Italian developer Gabriele Cirulli over a single weekend in March 2014. It was a hobby project, built quickly and released freely. The game drew on earlier sliding tile ideas that had been floating around the web, but Cirulli's version distilled the format to its cleanest form: a four by four grid, matching tiles merge, reach the 2048 tile. There was no company behind it, no marketing budget, and no plan for it to become what it became. That origin story matters, because it explains everything that happened next.

## Why It Spread So Fast

The game spread because it was free, because it was in the browser, and because it was open source. Anyone who received a link could play instantly with zero friction — no download, no account, no install. The rules took five seconds to understand, which made it perfect for sharing, and the difficulty curve meant players kept coming back to beat their own score. Because the code was released under the MIT license, other developers could copy, remix and republish it, which multiplied its reach far beyond the original site. Within its first week the game went from a hobby project to roughly four million visitors.

## The Genre It Created

Before 2048, the merge puzzle was a niche idea. After 2048, it was a genre. The game spawned countless variations: different grid sizes, different themes, different target tiles. The core loop — slide, merge, chase a bigger number — turned out to be endlessly reusable, and it remains popular more than a decade later. You can trace a direct line from that weekend project to the number puzzles, merge games and casual titles that fill app stores and browser game sites today. Few hobby projects can claim to have invented a category.

## What the Open Source Legacy Means Today

Because 2048 is open source under the MIT license, it can be hosted freely and legally by anyone, with credit to the original developer. That is why the version on this site exists: it is the same game that went viral in 2014, running in your browser with no ads inside the game, no account and no download. Your best score is saved locally, so you can close the tab and come back later. If you have never played it, or you only remember it from the app-store clones, the original experience is still worth a few minutes of your day.
