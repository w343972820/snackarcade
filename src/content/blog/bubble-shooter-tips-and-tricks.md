# Bubble Shooter Tips: How to Clear Levels Faster

Bubble Shooter looks like a game of aim and fire, but the players who clear levels fast are doing something else: they are solving a geometry puzzle with every shot. The cluster hangs from the ceiling, the launcher sits at the bottom, and each bubble you fire locks into place — either it pops a match or it becomes part of the problem. The difference between a slow game and a fast one is not luck. It is where you aim, which colour you choose, and what you leave hanging. These are the habits that make the ceiling disappear. If you need the basics first, the [Bubble Shooter](/games/bubble-shooter/) page covers the rules and controls; this guide assumes you can already aim and fire and want to clear levels faster.

## Aim Low, Not Into the Middle

The single biggest speed gain comes from aiming at the underside of the cluster instead of firing into its middle. A bubble that locks onto the bottom edge touches several neighbours at once, so a three-match down there can pop a whole hanging section — and the section drops instead of just disappearing in place. Firing into the middle of the cluster buries your bubble where it can only touch one or two others, and it usually blocks a gap you will need later. When you have a choice between a low shot and a middle shot, take the low one.

## Use the Walls for the Shots You Cannot Make

Straight shots can only reach the front of the cluster. The bubbles tucked behind others — the ones that keep a colour from ever matching — need a bank shot. The side walls bounce a fired bubble at a clean, predictable angle, so you can curve around the front row and hit the back of the cluster. Get into the habit of checking the walls before you give up on a colour: if a colour has three bubbles but one is hidden, a single wall bounce can turn an impossible colour into a match. It costs nothing to try, and the guide line shows you the full path before you commit.

## Play One Colour at a Time

The fastest clears are the tidy ones. Players who scatter shots across every colour leave a ceiling full of orphan bubbles — single blues, lone greens — with no match anywhere, and every one of those is a wasted shot later. Instead, pick the colour with the most bubbles visible and work it until it pops, then move to the next. While you do that, watch the launcher's next bubble: if the next one is the colour you are clearing, save the current shot for a useful position instead of firing it into a dead gap. Planning two shots ahead sounds slow, but it is exactly what makes the actual shots fast.

## Set Up Big Drops Instead of Small Matches

A three-match that pops in place is fine. A three-match that leaves a whole group of bubbles with no support is the real win, because the unsupported group falls and scores bonus points — sometimes clearing half the board in one drop. Look for matches at the bottom edge that cut off support: when you pop the bubbles holding up a cluster, gravity does the rest. If a colour is scattered and unsupported, leave it alone until you can pop its support in a single shot. The drop is worth far more than the match, and setting one up is the difference between clearing a level and scraping through it.

## Slow Down as the Ceiling Drops

The game's real difficulty is the line at the bottom. As the cluster hangs lower, the room for error shrinks, and this is exactly where fast players make slow decisions. Do not fire at the first angle that looks okay. Look for the match that removes the most bubbles, even if it means taking an extra second to line up a wall bounce. One careful shot that clears twenty bubbles beats three rushed shots that clear two each — and every rushed shot also risks locking a bubble into a spot that blocks the next one.

## Clear Your Next Level Faster

Open [Bubble Shooter](/games/bubble-shooter/) and play one round with one rule: aim low, and only fire at a colour you are actively clearing. Then add wall bounces for hidden bubbles, then start setting up drops instead of single matches. The ceiling comes down a lot faster when every shot has a job.
