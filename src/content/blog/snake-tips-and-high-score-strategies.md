# How to Play Snake: Rules, Tips, and High-Score Strategies

Snake looks like a game about reflexes, but every long game is really about planning. The snake never stops moving, so every turn is a commitment: one wrong direction and the run is over. The good news is that the rules fit in one sentence, and the habits that produce high scores are learnable patterns, not reflexes you are born with. This guide covers the rules quickly, then walks through the movement habits that keep games alive long enough for the score to climb. If you have not played yet, open [Snake](/games/snake/) and run one game first — it helps to feel the controls before you study them.

## The Rules, Refreshed

One rule decides everything: eat the food to grow and score, and the game ends the moment your head hits the wall or your own body. The snake cannot reverse directly into itself, so the only direction you cannot choose is the one you are already coming from. On desktop you steer with the arrow keys or WASD; on a phone you swipe. There is no timer, no lives and no continues — one run, one score, and your best score is saved locally so the next game has a target. That is the whole contract. Everything below is about living inside it longer.

## Start with a Loop, Not a Zigzag

The most common early-game mistake is chasing the food in tight zigzags. It works for the first ten pieces and then it paints you into a corner, because a zigzag fills the board with your own body in every direction. Instead, keep the snake moving in wide, predictable loops around the board — down one side, across the bottom, up the other side. A loop gives you two clear turning options at every moment and keeps the centre of the board open — the space you will need once the snake gets long. You will take slightly longer routes to the food, and that is exactly the point: in Snake, the fastest route is usually the deadliest one.

## Grow in Order, Not in Knots

Once the snake passes twenty or thirty segments, the score stops coming from eating and starts coming from not dying. A long snake is a wall you have to steer, so keep the body organised. The cleanest shape is a set of parallel lanes: the snake runs along one row, turns, runs back along the row next to it, turns again. As the head moves forward, the tail follows and clears the lane behind it — the body becomes a moving corridor instead of a tangle. Players who let their snake knot up die with food everywhere; players who hold parallel lanes keep moving for a very long time.

## Give Yourself a Turning Lane

This is the habit that separates long games from short ones: always keep at least a two-cell gap between the head and the nearest part of the body. When food appears behind you, that gap is the difference between a clean loop and a head-on collision. The easy check is to look at the two squares in front of the head and the two squares beside it. If any of them hold the body, you do not have a turn — you have a wall. When the board gets crowded, aim for the largest open area instead of the nearest food. The food will still be there; the open space will not.

## Use the Walls as a Tool, Not a Trap

Beginners treat the edges as a danger to avoid. Long-game players treat them as a tool. Sweeping the outer lanes early lets you collect food on long, predictable runs, and it keeps the centre clear for emergency turns later. The trick is to run the walls with intent: travel a full lane before you turn, and never hug the wall for a single square before cutting inward. When the board fills up, the outer lanes become your last safe corridor — the snake that practised wall runs all game is the one that survives the final minutes.

## Know When to Take a Risk

Risk in Snake is a simple calculation: how long is your snake, and how much space sits between you and the food? Early, when the snake is short and the board is mostly empty, you can cut across the middle for almost any food. Late in a game, the same shortcut is suicide — the body fills most of the board and the "shortcut" runs straight through it. The rule of thumb: if the direct line to the food passes within two cells of your body, take the long way. If the food sits in a corner and your snake is long, treat that corner as a trap and let the food wait until the tail clears a path.

## Try These Snake Strategies Now

Open [Play Snake](/games/snake/) and play one game with a single goal: keep the snake in wide loops for the first fifty points, no tight zigzags. Then add the turning-lane check, then the parallel-lane habit, then the wall runs. Each one builds on the last, and together they turn Snake from twitchy reactions into quiet planning. Your best score is saved automatically, so the next run already has a number to beat.
