# How to Play Klondike Solitaire: Rules for Beginners

Klondike Solitaire is the card game that most people have seen on a computer screen at some point but never actually learned properly. It looks like a pile of cards that shuffles itself, but underneath the green felt is a planning puzzle with clear rules and a single goal: move every card onto the four foundation piles, from Ace up to King in each suit. The version here at SnackArcade runs in your browser with no download and no sign-up, and you can [play Klondike Solitaire online](/games/klondike-solitaire/) the moment you finish reading. This guide walks through the rules in the order you actually need them.

## What You Are Trying to Do

The whole game is about the four foundation piles at the top of the table. Each foundation starts empty and must be built up in a single suit: the Ace of hearts first, then the 2, the 3, and so on up to the King of hearts. Do that for all four suits and you win. Everything else in the game — the seven tableau columns, the stock pile, the waste pile — exists to feed those four foundations. Keep that single goal in mind and the rules stop feeling random.

## The Tableau: Seven Columns of Cards

Below the foundations are seven columns called the tableau. The first column has one card, the second has two, and so on up to seven. Only the top card of each column is face up; the rest are hidden. You can move face-up cards around the tableau by placing a card on another card that is one rank higher and the opposite colour. A red 6 can go on a black 7, and a black 6 can go on a red 7. You build downward in alternating colours, and you can move a whole run of cards together if it is built correctly. Any empty tableau space can be filled by a King, and only by a King.

## The Stock and the Waste

When no useful tableau move is left, you draw from the stock pile in the corner. The card or cards you draw go to the waste pile, and the top card of the waste is playable. In draw-one mode you flip one card at a time. In draw-three mode you flip three at a time and only the top card of the three is available, which makes the game noticeably harder. ## Where the Strategy Actually Lives

Beginners treat Solitaire as luck, but most of the game is decision-making. The first rule of good play is to expose face-down cards. Every hidden card in the tableau is a card you cannot plan around, so the moves that turn over hidden cards are almost always the best moves. The second rule is to keep columns balanced — a column with ten cards and a column with two behave very differently, and long columns trap the cards you need. The third rule is to be careful with foundations: once a card is on a foundation it cannot come back, so do not move a card up until you are sure you will not need it to build a longer run below.

## Draw One vs Draw Three

If you are new, start with draw-one mode. It gives you full control over the stock and a much higher win rate, which makes it the right place to learn the patterns. Draw-three is the traditional scoring mode and the one serious Solitaire players use, but it demands more planning because the stock hides two cards between every available one. Play both and you will quickly feel how different the two games are, even on the same deal.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most common beginner mistake is filling a foundation too early and then discovering you needed that card to expose something underneath. Another is ignoring empty tableau spaces — a King belongs in an empty column because it clears the pile it came from, and empty columns are the most valuable real estate on the table. A third mistake is drawing from the stock out of habit instead of scanning the tableau for a move that uncovers a hidden card. Slow down, look at the whole table, and make the move that reveals the most.

## Why Solitaire Is Worth Learning

Solitaire is one of the few games that is equally good for a two-minute break and a long evening. It has no clock, no opponent and no way to be rushed, and a well-played deal is genuinely satisfying.
