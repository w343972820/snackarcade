Racing games in a browser have one job: get you from a standing start to something that feels fast within about four seconds. There is no garage to manage, no career mode to unlock and no forty-minute tutorial about tyre compounds. You press a key, the car moves, and either you take the corner or you don't.

This category covers top-down circuit racers, endless highway drivers where the road never ends, time-trial games built around one perfect lap, and physics-driven driving games where staying upright is the actual challenge. What they have in common is a control scheme you can learn while already moving.

Handling is the thing that makes or breaks a browser racing game, and it is almost never explained by the game itself. Every page in this category describes how that specific game steers — whether braking rotates the car, whether there is a drift mechanic hiding behind the handbrake, and whether letting go of the accelerator is ever the right move. That information usually takes ten wasted attempts to work out on your own.

Because these games depend on fast, uninterrupted input, each page also lists the practical details that affect play: whether key repeat matters, whether the game pauses when the tab loses focus, and which keys do nothing at all despite looking like they should.

Racing is a smaller category here than puzzle or card games, and deliberately so. The games in it are ones that hold up without a download, rather than everything that could technically be embedded.
