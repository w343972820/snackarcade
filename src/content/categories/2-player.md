Online multiplayer is great until you're sitting next to the person you want to play with. These games are built for exactly that situation: two people, one board, one device — no lobby, no room code and no waiting for a match to start.

Most of the collection is turn-based. Chess, Checkers, Four in a Row and Gomoku all work the same way: you share one mouse, one keyboard or one phone, the board shows whose turn it is, and you hand the device over when your move is done. There is no control scheme to divide up, because only one of you is playing at any single moment.

Paddle Ball is the exception. It is played in real time by two people at once, so it does use two sets of keys — player one on Q and A, player two on P and L — and it is the one game here that needs a keyboard rather than a touchscreen.

That difference is why every page in this category spells out the exact controls instead of assuming you already know a convention. The turn-based games say "click or tap your move"; Paddle Ball lists both key sets. You can sort out who is doing what before the first round rather than during it.

Every game here is head-to-head: two players, one winner. There are no co-op titles and no shared-goal modes in the collection. Sessions run anywhere from two minutes for a single board to twenty for a full game of chess.
