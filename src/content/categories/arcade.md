Arcade games were designed to take your money in ninety seconds. Everything about them — the escalating speed, the single life, the score counter ticking in the corner — exists to create a very specific feeling: that you were one mistake away from doing much better, and that the next attempt is the one. That design has aged remarkably well, because a game built to be understood in ninety seconds is exactly the kind of game you want in a browser tab.

This category covers stacking and falling-block games, dodge-and-survive games, single-screen chase games, and the whole family of high-score chasers where there is no ending, only a number. What they share is a one-sentence rule set and a difficulty curve that does the teaching for you.

Nothing here is an official release of a trademarked arcade title. These are open-source remakes and original games written in the same spirit, and every page credits the developer who actually wrote the code with a link back to their repository and licence. That matters more in this category than any other, because retro arcade names are among the most aggressively protected trademarks in the industry.

Because arcade games live or die on input responsiveness, each page documents the full control scheme including the shortcuts people usually miss — pause, restart, hard drop, soft drop, and whether key repeat works the way you expect when you hold a direction down.

New arcade titles are added regularly. Sessions run short by design, so this is the category to open when you have five minutes rather than fifty.
