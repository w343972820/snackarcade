  about:
    - >-
      Checkers — also called draughts — is one of the oldest board games still played today,
      with variants found across the world for centuries. It looks simpler than chess, but
      it is a genuine strategy game: the first player to understand the power of the king
      and the value of position will beat a player who just jumps at every capture.
    - >-
      The game is played on the dark squares of an eight by eight board. Pieces move one
      square diagonally forward, and capture by jumping over an adjacent opponent piece to
      the empty square beyond. A piece that reaches the far side is crowned a king, which
      can then move diagonally in both directions — kings are the heart of the endgame.
    - >-
      This version is based on an open-source draughts game by gartz, released under the MIT
      licence. It runs entirely in your browser and supports two players sharing one screen.
      No download, no sign-up and no waiting for an opponent — just deal the board and play.

  howToPlay:
    - step: "Set up the board."
      detail: >-
        Each player places twelve pieces on the dark squares of their side of the board.
        Red moves first, then players alternate turns.
    - step: "Move diagonally forward."
      detail: >-
        A piece moves one square diagonally forward onto an empty dark square. Pieces can
        never move or land on the light squares.
    - step: "Capture by jumping."
      detail: >-
        If an opponent's piece is diagonally adjacent and the square beyond is empty, you
        must jump over it to capture it. Multiple jumps in one turn are allowed.
    - step: "Crown your kings."
      detail: >-
        A piece that reaches the opponent's back row becomes a king. Kings move diagonally
        in both directions, which makes them far more powerful.
    - step: "Win the game."
      detail: >-
        You win by capturing all of your opponent's pieces or by leaving them with no legal
        move. The game ends the moment either condition is met.

  tips:
    - title: "Move toward the crown row"
      body: >-
        The strongest pieces are kings, and kings come from reaching the far side of the
        board. Every move that pushes a man toward the crown row is a move toward a more
        powerful army.
    - title: "Do not take every capture"
      body: >-
        Capturing is sometimes forced, but when you have a choice, think twice. A capture
        that moves your piece into a vulnerable square is often worse than passing up a
        piece.
    - title: "Control the centre"
      body: >-
        Pieces in the centre of the board have more mobility and more options. A piece
        trapped on the edge can only move in one direction, while a centre piece can pick
        its battles.
    - title: "Build a defensive wall"
      body: >-
        Keeping your pieces in a connected formation makes it hard for the opponent to jump
        through them. A single isolated piece is a target; a row of pieces is a wall.
    - title: "Trade pieces when you are ahead"
      body: >-
        If you are winning on material, exchanging pieces simplifies the position and makes
        your advantage easier to convert into a win.
    - title: "Watch for double jumps"
      body: >-
        Before you move, check whether your move opens a double jump for your opponent.
        One careless step can hand them two captures and the game.
